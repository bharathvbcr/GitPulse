GitPulse — long video narration (about 4 min, 14 scenes)
For Google or Grok TTS. One block per scene; the time is where the scene sat in the last recording.
Pace target: about 150 words per minute.
Every sentence here has footage in the recorded takes. Scenes 4, 6, 9 and 10 were rewritten on
2026-11-17 to match what was filmed: no Codex launch, no quit-and-relaunch, no browser pane.

[Scene 1 · 0:00–0:12 · Cold open]
This is what coding looks like now. Agents running in parallel, across every repository you own,
and one person keeping it all on track. GitPulse is where you run all of it.

[Scene 2 · 0:12–0:28 · The problem]
Most agent tools give you a chat window per agent. But agents collide. They wander outside their
checkout. They melt your laptop. And a transcript won't show you any of that.
GitPulse shows the work itself, to you and to your agents.

[Scene 3 · 0:28–0:49 · One workspace]
Every repository gets a tab. Every checkout of a repository stacks into that tab, so worktrees
never get lost. Fleet compares all of your repositories at a glance: what's changed, what's out of
sync, and what's unhealthy. And the Overview puts what's blocked first, like a merge waiting on you,
so you always know where you're needed.

[Scene 4 · 0:49–1:10 · Task to agent]
Work starts on the task board. Write a task, and let Manvi tighten the wording. Then launch.
Pick the agent, the model and the effort. GitPulse creates a fresh worktree on a branch of its own,
so the agent's work stays apart from your checkout until you choose to merge it.

[Scene 4b · 1:10–1:25 · Agents fill the board]
When an agent finds something outside its task, it doesn't bury it in a transcript. It files it on
your board, and GitPulse checks it isn't a duplicate first. Nothing gets lost.

[Scene 5 · 1:25–1:55 · Control plane]
The Agents view shows every session as a tree, parents and the children they started. GitPulse sees
Claude Code, Codex and Antigravity sessions, even the ones it didn't launch.
Now two agents are about to edit the same file. GitPulse warns before they collide, and the agents
can message each other through the peer inbox instead of overwriting each other's work.
And Follow agent opens each file the agent edits, live, right beside its terminal.

[Scene 6 · 1:55–2:17 · Guards]
Claude Code and Codex shell commands pass through Manvi's policy gate before they run. Here, an
agent's shell command tries to write outside its repository, and the gate stops it.
Repositories are trusted one at a time, and only by you. GitPulse scans its own commits for secrets,
and stops the commit before it happens. And when an agent runs git, GitPulse snapshots first, so you
can review the change and undo it.

[Scene 7 · 2:17–2:43 · DevMap]
DevMap is a code graph of your repository. Explore it by subsystem, file or symbol. Open a diff and
see its blast radius: everything the change can reach. Then run local CI on just the tests it
affects, with the full suite as the fallback whenever the graph isn't sure. Your agents get the same
answers, through the same tools.

[Scene 8 · 2:43–2:59 · Load scheduler]
Parallel builds don't have to mean a frozen machine. The load scheduler gives heavy builds slots and
queues the rest, and you can park an idle agent with one click.
The live load strip shows CPU, GPU, memory and thermal pressure as it happens.

[Scene 9 · 2:59–3:21 · Terminal]
The terminal is built for power users. Split it into rows and columns, and group your splits.
Every command is its own block, with its exit status and how long it took,
and you can copy a command and its output in one click.

[Scene 10 · 3:21–3:35 · Git, fully]
And it's a complete git client. A commit graph with avatars. Stacked branches as a tree, restacked
when their base moves. A three-way conflict editor. Pull requests reviewed and merged, and your
workflows, without leaving the window.

[Scene 11 · 3:35–3:40 · Insights]
Pulse, coverage, health and storage, all computed on your machine.

[Scene 12 · 3:40–3:43 · Make it yours]
Seventeen themes, your own wallpapers, and a command palette for everything.

[Scene 13 · 3:43–3:56 · Free, Labs, next]
GitPulse is free and open source, and it runs on your machine. In Labs: remote runners on your own
machines, a secrets vault, device sync, team controls, and integrations with Jira, Trello and more.

[Scene 14 · 3:56–4:02 · Call to action]
